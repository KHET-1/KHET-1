1. Executive Summary

This report presents findings from forensic network analysis of packet capture data recorded from the operator's workstation. The analysis was conducted to identify the source, mechanism, and scope of persistent unauthorized data transmission from a machine used for sensitive legal and patent work.

Primary Finding: The Grok (xAI) web application's Progressive Web App (PWA) service worker maintained a persistent, high-volume data pipeline from the operator's machine to xAI servers. This pipeline continued transmitting at full rate (12,000–37,000 byte TLS 1.3 Application Data payloads) for more than 13 hours after the operator closed the visible browser tab. The transmitted data included privileged case material — standards documents, patent drafts, and KHET-1 operation notes — being actively worked on during the captured sessions.

Mechanism: PWA background service worker (sync/fetch handlers) — a deliberate architectural choice by xAI that keeps the web client "awake" in the background for instant resume capability. Side effect: continuous transmission of session data without ongoing user visibility or explicit consent.

Assessment: This is not malware, not a hack, and not accidental. It is intentional xAI client architecture. However, the effect is that privileged work product was silently transmitted to a third-party AI company's servers without the operator's knowledge or ongoing consent, for an extended duration after active use ceased.
